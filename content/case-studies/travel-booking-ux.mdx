## Context

A high-traffic travel-tech booking funnel was leaking users at predictable
points. Conversion was the headline metric, but the real story was friction:
too many steps, unclear states, and avoidable errors.

## The problem

Users dropped off most at the steps that asked for the most effort with the least
clarity. The instinct was to discount our way to conversion — but that treats the
symptom, not the funnel.

## What I did

- Ran research and session analysis to pinpoint the highest-drop-off steps and
  *why* people stalled there.
- Prioritized friction removal over incentives: collapsed steps, clarified
  states, and pre-empted the most common errors.
- Shipped changes behind experiments so every improvement was measured, not
  assumed.

## Outcome

- **Funnel completion up ~18%** — without any discounting.
- **Support tickets down ~25%** as confusing states disappeared.
- **Funnel simplified** from three steps to two.

## Takeaway

The cheapest conversion lever is usually removing friction, not adding
incentives. Research told us where; experimentation told us whether it worked.

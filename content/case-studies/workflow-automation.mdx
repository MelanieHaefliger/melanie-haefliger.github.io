## Before & after

Every volunteering application at Benevity touched three separate tools — Slack, ClickUp, and Salesforce — and someone had to do all the connecting manually.

## How I approached it

**First, I mapped every step.** I sat with the support and CS team and documented exactly what happened from application to resolution. Eight steps. Six of them manual and repetitive.

**Then I defined the data layer.** The three tools spoke different languages. I mapped which fields in Salesforce corresponded to which fields in ClickUp, and where Slack fit in. That field mapping became the foundation — without it, nothing could automate correctly.

**Then we rolled it out in phases.** Starting with the highest-volume pain points: auto-create the ticket, auto-fill the fields, trigger the first email. Measured the impact. Then expanded to the monitoring and status-update layer.

We phased it because we had limited engineering capacity — not every automation could go live at once, so we prioritised the highest-impact steps first.

## Outcome

**Before:** each incoming request took 5–10 minutes of support time just to handle the initial ticket — create it, fill in the fields, email the right people.

**After:** ~0 minutes. Support only gets tagged in two cases: a required custom field is missing, or the user follows up after the automated response. Everything else runs without a human touching it.

**70% reduction in manual workload overall** — starting at ~40% after the first release, rising to 70% across later iterations. ~15 support and CS staff moved to higher-value work. 5,000+ users started getting faster, more consistent responses.

> The thing I remember most: before automation, people spent hours just trying to find out what was happening with a request. After, the answer was always in ClickUp — because the system put it there automatically.

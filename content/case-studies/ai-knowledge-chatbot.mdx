## Context

Support and internal teams at Benevity needed answers that lived across Slack,
Drive, and Confluence. Finding them meant searching three places or pinging a
colleague — and the same questions came up again and again.

## The problem

An internal AI knowledge chatbot could answer these instantly, but only if it was
trustworthy: grounded in the right sources, honest about where answers came from,
and respectful of who's allowed to see what.

## What I did — the product decisions

- **Source scoping** — decided which Slack, Drive, and Confluence sources to
  index, balancing coverage against noise and stale content.
- **Response & citation behaviour** — defined how the assistant should answer and
  require citations, so users could verify rather than blindly trust.
- **Retrieval-time access policy** — enforced permissions at retrieval, so the bot
  never surfaced content a user wasn't entitled to see.
- **Post-launch iteration** — used real usage to tune sources and behaviour.

## Outcome

- **Support tickets cut by 10%** as the assistant deflected repeat questions.
- A reusable pattern for grounding an LLM in internal knowledge **without leaking
  access or trust.**

## Takeaway

> The hard part of an AI feature isn't the model — it's the trust scaffolding:
> citations, source scoping, and access control.

That belief now carries directly into my own AI builds.

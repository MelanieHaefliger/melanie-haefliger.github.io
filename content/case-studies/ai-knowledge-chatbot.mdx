## The problem

Support and internal teams at Benevity spent too much time hunting for answers that already existed somewhere — buried in Confluence docs or a Slack thread from three months ago.

The same questions came up again and again. Finding an answer meant either searching across multiple tools, or pinging a colleague who might not respond for hours.

## What we built

Two surfaces, one knowledge layer: a Slack assistant for quick internal Q&A, and a Zendesk integration that surfaced suggested answers and related knowledge base articles directly inside open tickets.

Both were grounded in the same indexed sources — Confluence, Slack, Google Workspace — with citations shown so employees could verify before acting.

If an answer was wrong, a source was missing, or the response didn't satisfy, employees could rate it and leave a comment. That feedback drove decisions on which content to index and helped us identify gaps in the knowledge base over time.

## Outcome

**Support employees spent 30% less time on tickets on average.** The main driver was the Zendesk integration: suggested answers and relevant KB articles appeared in context while agents worked a ticket, so they didn't have to leave the tool, search Confluence, or ping a colleague to find the right answer.

The LLM underneath started as OpenAI GPT and later migrated under a different project owner, but the decisions about sources, access, and citations stayed the same throughout.

> The hard part of an AI feature isn't the model — it's making it trustworthy. I decided what it could see, when to show it, and who could access what. That belief now carries directly into every AI build I do.
